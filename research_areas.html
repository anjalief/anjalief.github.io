Scope of this page
This page is intended to provide some context to potential students on what I work on and what I expect from students. You can also find information on my lab website, which may be more up to date. If you are a prospective PhD student interested in working with me, I encourage you to apply to JHU (information here) and include my name on your application.

Are you taking students?
I will take 0-1 new PhD students for Fall 2027.


If I am a prospective PhD student, should I email you?
Please do not email me. Instead, apply (here) and list my name as a faculty you are interested in working with. I do read applications that mention my name, and I have found that emails have effectively no impact on admissions decisions. Thus, as a rule I generally do not reply, because I believe that replying is more likely to mislead applicants into over-estimating the impact of their email than to accomplish anything else.


I'm interested in working with you as an MS, undergrad, or high school student.
If you are currently a JHU graduate or undergraduate student interested in working with my lab, feel free to reach out to me!
I generally do not work with high school students or undergrads from other universities except through structured programs. You can view JHU programs for high school students here.


Research Areas
My primary field of research is natural language processing, also known as AI/data science with text and speech data. Within this space, I focus on AI ethics and computational social science. My current focus falls under three categories:


Large-scale text and speech corpus analysis
Can we use automated text processing tools to help us identify and understand social phenomena that manifest in language? While tools for processing short-form data like a sentence or paragraph are increasingly capable, our ability to extract patterns from large-scale corpora remains limited. This line of work often involves developing new technology that facilitates open-ended text analysis and sometimes involves actually conducting those analyses, often in partnership with social scientists. The long-term impact goals of these projects often focus on informing policy decisions. Our current application domains are focused on public health and policing contexts. In the past I have also conducted projects focused on propaganda, misinformation, and stereotypes and bias. Some papers in this space include:
  • Mian Zhong, Katherine A. Keith, Anjalie Field. "Exploring Sparse Autoencoders in Text-Based Causal Confounding Adjustment". EMNLP (2026) (text)
  • Robert Cole Molloy, Stephen L. Morgan, Rhiannon N. Miller, Anjalie Field. "Conducting Reliable Keyword Estimation with Unreliable ASR in Police Audio Data". SLT (2026)
  • Kaavya Chaparala, Su Huang, Stephen L. Morgan, Rhiannon N. Miller, Anjalie Field. "Pretrained ASR Pseudo-labeling for Noisy Police Audio". SLT (2026) (text)
  • Mian Zhong*, Pristina Wang*, and Anjalie Field. "HICode: Hierarchical Inductive Coding with LLMs" EMNLP (2025) (text)


AI Privacy
Much research involving analysis of large-scale datasets requires access to highly sensitive data like healthcare records, social services notes, and policing footage. Furthermore, generative AI has introduced a number of new privacy risks, including increased ability to infer sensitive attributes about people and tendencies to share personal information with models. How can we uncover and mitigate these risks? And how can we enable research in high-stakes domains without introducing privacy violations? Our work on these topics involves a combination of developing text processing methods with formal privacy guarantees, empirical investigations, and human-centered approaches to understanding privacy.
  • Krithika Ramesh, Krishna Pillutla, Danish Pruthi, Anjalie Field. "The Privacy-Hallucination Tradeoff in Differentially Private Language Models". Findings of EMNLP (2026) (text)
  • Pulkit Madaan, Krithik Ramesh, Lisa Bauer, Charith Peris, Anjalie Field. Multi-Token Completion for Text Anonymization. EACL (2026) (text)
  • Yaxuan Ren, Krithika Ramesh, Yaxing Yao, and Anjalie Field. "How do we measure privacy in text? A survey of text anonymization metrics" Findings of AACL (2025) (text)
  • Zihao Zhao and Anjalie Field. "Controlled Generation for Private Synthetic Text" EMNLP (2025) (text)


User-centered AI evaluation
How are AI systems actually performing for real people in real deployed settings? Are all users able to benefit from AI equally? If not, what drives those differences? Our work in this space focuses on analyzing AI usage patterns and how those patterns impact outputs, with a focus on real use cases and deployed settings.
  • Katherine Van Koevering, Anjalie Field. "It’s How You Ask: Gender-Associated Linguistic Bias in LLMs". COLM (2026) (text)
  • Alina Hyk, Kiera McCormick, Mian Zhong, Ioana Ciucă, Sanjib Sharma, John F. Wu, J. E. G. Peek, Kartheik G. Iyer, Ziang Xiao, Anjalie Field. "From Queries to Criteria: Understanding How Astronomers Evaluate LLMs." COLM (2025) (text)


These categories are not independent: if we want to understand how AI models are impacting society, we need methods to analyze large scale usage data without introducing privacy violations.

Culture, Values, and Expectations
I am primarily looking for students who are highly motivated to work on social issues. While I do not always expect incoming students to have extensive experience in NLP, students should be comfortable writing code. Students from interdisciplinary backgrounds are welcome. I have had lots of very productive collaborations with people in a variety of fields and institutions, including sociology, political science, economics, statistics, etc.

I will strive to maintain a lab that values inclusiveness and is a safe environment for all members. I work closely with students, including regular meetings (e.g. weekly) and active involvement in projects, such as helping with paper writing. Students who are looking for hands-on involvement and close collaboration are a good fit. While I do expect frequent communication and regular progress, I have no intention of tracking students' work hours or vacation time, and students will be encouraged to set schedules that best support their mental health and productivity.
